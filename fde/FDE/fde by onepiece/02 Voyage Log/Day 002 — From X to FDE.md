### 🏝️ Location 1 — Assess existing full-stack and infrastructure skills

#### 🎭 Journal Scene — The Cargo Manifest

Jinbe spreads the résumés on the driftwood bench. "Before a long voyage," he says, "a helmsman inventories the hold. Not to despair at what's missing — to *provision deliberately*. Zoro. Your old sea?"

"A dojo. Ten thousand swings a day. Then more."

"And what does an FDE crew get from that?"

Zoro thinks about it the way he thinks about sword angles. "A method. Brute force first, measure, then optimize. I don't quit problems." Pause. "I'm useless at talking to clients until the third try."

"Then that row is written honestly: *carries a practice method — evidence: he refactor-cut Day 1's prototype; gap: customer communication — closing artifact: one training problem written for learners, not himself.*" Jinbe writes it. "Next."

Franky: "Shipwright! Modularity, blueprints, load-bearing—" he wilts slightly under Jinbe's gaze, "…and a documented tendency to build fourteen arrows when two hold the weight."

"Which you corrected yourself yesterday, in front of witnesses. That's evidence of a *closing* gap, not an open one."

Luffy, when his turn comes, holds up the meat drawing. Everyone waits. "I'm good at *starting* things," he says at last, cheerfully and completely without shame. "I need Nami for the *stopping* and the *shaping*."

"That," Jinbe says, writing, "may be the most useful self-assessment I have heard this year."

The audit takes the morning. Nami pins the finished chart — **SKILLS_AUDIT.md**, the crew's first portfolio artifact — to the palm tree. (It now lives in the project repo, exactly as written that morning.)

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"Before you go shopping for adventure supplies, you look in the hold! There's already rice in there! There's already rope! You don't buy more rope, you buy the *thing you actually don't have* — which yesterday was 'a plan,' which is why Nami bought a whole plan. The manifest isn't a report card, it's a **shopping list written by someone honest**. And the trick is the last column: stuff you decide NOT to buy on purpose. Jinbe's not buying 'modern consoles.' He says his partner handles those while he handles the *why*."

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** a ship's hold contains objects that stay useful forever; skills decay and interlock — your Python matters differently depending on whether you also have discovery skills. And cargo has no ego: an audit's hardest job is honest evidence, because humans pad their hold with things they *attended* rather than things they *shipped*. The exact technical distinction: **a skills audit is evidence-based asset mapping — each strength must cite an artifact, incident, or metric; each gap must name the artifact that closes it (not a course that "covers" it); and it must include a deliberate won't-learn list, because an audit without exclusions is a wish list, not a plan.**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

The audit table (run it for yourself, five columns):

| Column | Question | Quality bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prior role / project | Where did you actually sail? | Non-engineering roles count — *especially* for FDE (support, consulting, analysis teach customer truth) |
| Carries over | What transfers, **with evidence**? | "Python" is weak; "built the intake script that processed 300 tickets/week (ran 6 months)" is an audit row |
| Gap for FDE work | What's missing that this job needs? | Three at a time, maximum. Twenty gaps is zero gaps |
| Closing artifact | What *thing you can show* closes it? | An artifact, not a certificate: a repo, a decision log, a measured result, a post-mortem |
| Deliberately won't learn | What are you *choosing* not to close? | Named and owned — you'll partner or hire for it instead |

For FDE specifically, the audit maps against four skill families: **full-stack breadth** (build a thing end-to-end), **infrastructure** (run the thing where the work happens), **customer discovery** (find the real workflow), and **production ownership** (keep it alive, hand it over). Most career-changers arrive strong in one or two and empty in the others — the audit says *which ones*, and the roadmap's islands are the closing plan.

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️ (advanced):** the audit's failure mode is the ** résumé-driven inventory** — listing technologies instead of capabilities. "Kubernetes" on a scroll is not a capability; "containerized a service and wrote the rollback procedure I used twice" is. The test for every row: *can a stranger verify it without talking to you?* If not, it's a belief, not an asset. Second trap: auditing only *up* (new tech) and never *across* (writing, scoping, stakeholding) — FDE careers die of the across-gaps far more often than the up-gaps.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Dissect one real row from the crew's shipped audit (`SKILLS_AUDIT.md`, in the repo — this is a real artifact, not an illustration):

> **Usopp | Village watchman | Risk lists, edge-case instinct — *evidence: Day 1 corrupt-file test + atomic-write guardrail* | Calibrating panic to probability | A failure-mode table with honest likelihoods**

Layer by layer:

1. **The evidence is dated and checkable.** "Day 1 corrupt-file test" points at `test_corrupt_json_raises_clear_error` — you can run it. A stranger with the repo can verify the row without trusting Usopp's self-image (which, at 8,000 exaggerated problems, requires verifying).
2. **The gap is small and behavioral.** Not "learn statistics" — "calibrate likelihoods," closable by one specific artifact (the failure-mode table). Gaps shaped like artifacts get closed; gaps shaped like subjects get scrolled.
3. **The won't-learn column holds the plan's honesty.** Jinbe's row ("modern tooling fluency — pairs instead, documents the *why*") converts a weakness into a *working style*. That conversion is a senior move available on day one of your transition.
4. **Remove a layer and watch it rot:** evidence-free strengths inflate into Diego's cart; artifact-free gaps never close (nothing to finish); no won't-learn list and the audit quietly promises everything, which is the same as promising nothing.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

> **Common Failure Mode (from the syllabus, verbatim):** *"Collecting certificates without evidence of delivering under customer constraints."*

- **Symptom:** a wall of completions; no repo, no decision log, no measured result; interviews stall at "tell me about a time a customer…" 
- **Root cause:** auditing by *consumption* (hours in, scrolls out) instead of by *delivery* (constraints met, systems shipped); often downstream of choosing courses before reading the harbor rules (Location 5's exact antidote).
- **Mitigation:** the audit method itself — every claimed capability must cite an artifact; every gap gets a closing artifact; certificates may appear only as *scaffolding* inside a row ("used course X to build Y"), never as the row itself.
- **Completion Evidence (specific, observable):** your audit exists, a stranger can verify each row's evidence without you, and at least one row's closing artifact is *finished*.

**Trade-offs:** depth-first (close one gap with one big artifact) vs. breadth-first (three small artifacts) — depth wins interviews for senior roles, breadth survives ambiguous junior FDE pipelines; pick per the job descriptions you actually have (Location 5). Time-box the audit itself: half a day. An audit that takes a week *is* procrastination wearing a clipboard (Nami's words, price: one week of tangerines).

### 🏝️ Location 2 — Practice ambiguity reduction

#### 🎭 Journal Scene — "The Door Thing Again"

Midday. A village kid — Pokkà, twelve, official messenger of the maintenance crew — jogs up with the day's requests on a scrap of paper. He reads aloud, carefully:

*"The door thing again. It's real bad this time. Also maybe the rope? — Chabo."*

The crew stares at the paper. The paper stares back.

"Which door," says Franky, "of the forty-one doors on this island."

"The *bad* door," says Pokkà, helpfully.

"Is 'the rope' the well rope, the gate rope, or a *metaphor*?" Usopp asks, already sketching failure trees.

Luffy, delighted: "I love this game! Is it a *fighting* door?!"

Zoro stands, walks over, and does something nobody expects: he asks **one question**. "Pokkà. When you say 'real bad' — what happens if nobody fixes it by tomorrow?"

Pokkà thinks. "Auntie Makino says the cold-store door won't shut, and the fish go bad, and then Uncle gets mad and *takes it out of the crew's pay*."

The beach goes quiet. In one answer: the door (cold-store), the severity (food spoils = money lost), the deadline (tomorrow), and the stakeholder (the uncle who docks pay). Zoro sits back down. "That's the question. Ask what happens if it's never fixed. Everything else falls out."

Nami is already writing it on the chart as an intake rule. Chopper, quietly: "That's… exactly how I diagnose. 'Where does it hurt *when you do the thing you need to do*?'"

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"You know a useless weather report? 'Weather's bad!' BAD *HOW*? Bad where? For how long? Do I need a coat or do I need to not sail AT ALL?! Now the good report: 'Storm, northwest, two hours, big waves.' SAME storm — but now Nami can DO something. Ambiguity reduction is turning 'the door thing is bad' into 'cold-store door won't shut; fish spoil by tomorrow; Makino's uncle fines the crew.' One you can shrug at. The other you can *fix by sunset*. It's not more information — it's the SAME information, sharpened!"

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** weather is a natural system you merely report better; requests come from a *person* who has reasons (shame, hurry, habit) for being vague — so ambiguity reduction is a conversation, not a rewrite, and the sharper version must come out of *their* mouth to stick. The exact technical distinction: **ambiguity reduction replaces an unfalsifiable statement with a decision-ready one by fixing its referents (which door?), its severity (what breaks, for whom?), and its horizon (by when?) — and the evidence bar is that a second person, reading only the restatement, would take the same action as the requester would.**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

Every vague request gets sharpened against three axes — the **RAR triad** (Referent, Impact, Horizon):

| Axis | The vague version | The question that fixes it | The door thing, sharpened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Referent** | "the door thing" | *Which* one, specifically? (Walk me to it.) | The cold-store door on the fish market's north wall |
| **Impact** | "it's real bad" | *What happens if it's never fixed — for whom?* | Fish spoil overnight; the crew's pay gets docked by the stall owner |
| **Horizon** | — | *By when does that happen?* | By tomorrow morning's catch |

The **impact question is the keystone** ("what happens if it's never fixed?"): it resolves referent and severity in the requester's *own* words, and it cannot be answered with "bad." Zoro's one question. Chopper's diagnostic twin. Note what the triad is *not*: it is not "ask more questions" — three precise axes beat twenty vague ones, and the difference shows up in the artifact (tonight's `--impact` field exists because the triad produces a *writable sentence*).

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️ (advanced):** ambiguity has *sources*, and they need different fixes: **ignorance** (the requester doesn't know — fix with the impact question), **conflict** (two stakeholders want different doors fixed — fix by surfacing the conflict, never by averaging it into mush), and **strategic vagueness** (someone benefits from an unclear scope — fix by writing the restatement down and asking for confirmation, which converts fog into a commitment device). The advanced reflex: when a restatement gets quietly *resisted*, you've found either a conflict or strategy — both are discoveries, not failures. And the system-level move is to reduce ambiguity *structurally*: intake forms, required fields, templates — tonight's build is exactly that, moved from conversation into the tool.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Reverse-engineer the scene: one question did the work of ten. Why that one?

1. **Form of the question.** "What happens if it's never fixed?" is a **counterfactual** — it forces a concrete future story ("fish spoil, uncle fines us") instead of an adjective ("bad"). Adjectives are unverifiable; stories contain referents, victims, and clocks.
2. **Data flow of the answer.** Pokkà's reply arrives *pre-structured*: harm → affected party → deadline. That structure is the RAR triad, generated free of charge, because the question's shape predetermined the answer's shape. Good questions are parsers.
3. **Why Zoro's version beat Franky's.** Franky asked "which door" (referent only — answerable as "the bad one," a loop). Zoro's question makes vagueness *expensive to maintain*: you cannot answer "what happens" with "it's bad" without sounding silly to yourself.
4. **In a real codebase/org:** this exact pattern lives in mature issue trackers as *required templates* — "Expected behavior / Actual behavior / Impact / Deadline" — and in incident response as impact-first triage. When you meet one, read it as fossilized ambiguity-reduction: somebody got tired of asking Zoro's question manually.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

- **Simplest workable approach:** the RAR triad, asked out loud, recorded in the requester's words.
- **Real alternative:** the structured-intake tool (required fields — tonight's `--impact`): shifts the work from the asker to the tool; costs design effort and can *suppress* reporting (forms are friction) — which is why Day 1's "copy one command from a card" bar still governs.
- **Trade-off table:**

| Decision | Lean | Cost accepted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ask vs. require (form fields) | Ask first; require only what proves load-bearing | Some quests still arrive vague |
| Whose words record the impact | The requester's | Crew can't "translate" it into jargon later |
| When to sharpen | At intake, not at triage time | Slightly slower to log |

- **Failure modes:** *premature precision* — sharpening with your own guesses instead of their answers (you build the wrong door, precisely); *interrogation feel* — fifteen questions where one impact question carries all three axes; *sharpened-then-ignored* — the restatement lives in a chat log, not the artifact, so triage re-reads fog. Symptom → fix for each: verify the restatement with the requester; count your questions; sharpen *into the record*, not the conversation.

#### ⚓ The Crew Debate — "Do we force impact notes on every quest?"

- **USOPP:** "Required field! With a minimum length! And a format! And—" *(Usopp. The form is a wall now. Sit.)*
- **SANJI:** "Optional, invited. The prompt asks beautifully, one line: 'what breaks if this is never done?' People *want* to tell you — if asking is a pleasure, not a toll."

### 🏝️ Location 3 — Build customer-facing artifacts

#### 🎭 Journal Scene — The Stamped Passage

Late afternoon. Diploma Diego, rebuffed but persistent, has set up his cart near the fish market. A different pitch now: "Sir! Madam! Would you like your crew's qualifications *certified*? A seal! A ribbon! A—"

Makino, behind her counter, cuts him off with the polite finality of someone who has run a shop for years. "Sir, when the fishmongers hired the carpenter last spring, they didn't ask for his ribbons. They walked to the *bridge he built* and stood on it."

She turns to the crew. "You know what the village has from you? Two days of quests that don't wash away in the rain. That's… actually astonishing? Woop Slap wants it *written up* — properly — for the village record. Can you do that? Something a stranger could read and understand what happened here?"

Brook appears at her elbow with terrifying speed for a skeleton. "Madam. I have been waiting my entire afterlife for someone to ask me that."

By dusk there are two new pages in the project chest: the crew audit (morning's chart, cleaned) and a **case study of the engagement so far** — what was decided, under what constraints, with what evidence. The village stamp sits in the corner of each. Makino stamped them herself. "For the record," she says. "So the next crew knows what was already done — and so do *you*, when you forget."

Jinbe, quietly, to Zoro: "*That* is the difference from Diego's cart. His scrolls say someone attended. These pages say something **happened** — and the customer holds the proof."

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"When you cross a sea, the port stamps your passage in the ship's log — and here's the important part — *the log stays on the ship*, and everyone who boards later can see: this crew really went there, really carried that, really came back. A customer-facing artifact is that stamp! It's not for *you* to feel proud — it's proof that lives with **somebody else** and still works when you're not standing there explaining it! Diego's scrolls only prove he *sat in rooms*. The stamp proves a *voyage*."

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** a port stamp is granted by authority; an artifact's force comes from *verifiability*, not endorsement — a repo a stranger can run beats a ribbon nobody can check. And the customer doesn't just *hold* the artifact; in FDE work the customer often *depends* on it operationally (the case study becomes the handover's ancestor). The exact technical distinction: **a customer-facing artifact is evidence designed for someone who wasn't there: it names the stakeholders' decisions, the constraints honored, the measured result, and the honest failures — verifiable without the author present. Certificates verify attendance; artifacts verify delivery.**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

The FDE portfolio's four artifact species (the syllabus's *Evidence of Mastery* is literally this list):

| Artifact | What it proves | Dawn Island instance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Working system** | You can ship, not just describe | Sunny Desk v0.1.1 — clone, run, two commands |
| **Decision log** | You chose under constraints, and can say why | `CASE_STUDY.md` §5: JSON-store, stdlib-only, `next_id`, optional-impact |
| **Measured result** | The world changed, and you checked | §4: 3 quests, 3 villager-logged vs 0 crew; baseline 0 written |
| **Honest failure analysis** | You can be trusted with bad news | §6: the demo flop, the alignment debt, the sample-size confession |

Rules that keep artifacts honest: **written for a stranger** (Brook's bar: "readable in the lamplight of someone who owes you nothing"); **evidence-linked** (every claim points at something runnable or countable); **failure included** (a case study without a failure section is marketing, and reviewers smell it); **customer-stamped where possible** (a client quote, a UAT sign-off, a village stamp — the proof that outlives your attendance).

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️ (advanced):** the subtle species is the **decision log** — the artifact interviewers trust most, because it can't be faked quickly. A real one records the *rejected alternatives* with the reason at the time (not retro-fitted wisdom), and it accumulates *in the moment* — which is why the crew's decision entries are dated (D1-01… D2-01) and why PROJECT STATE exists as a living ledger rather than a memoir. Practice note: write the decision the *day* you make it; a decision log written the night before the interview is a novel.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Dissect `CASE_STUDY.md` §6 ("Honest failure analysis") — the section amateurs skip:

> *"3 quests in 2 days proves the mechanism works, not that the village changed. The case study says so — an adoption claim stronger than the instrument would be a lie with charts."*

1. **What the sentence is doing:** pre-emptively *downgrading* its own evidence to the instrument's actual resolution. That's not humility theater — it's calibration, and reviewers raise hiring flags *for* it, not against it.
2. **Why it sits next to the successes:** the failure and the metric share a section so each prices the other. Success without a neighboring failure section reads as censored; failure without the metric reads as carelessness. Together: an instrument with a stated margin of error.
3. **The blast-radius test:** delete §6 and the case study still *sounds* fine — that's the danger. Every artifact needs a section whose deletion is undetectable to a skimmer but glaring to an expert; that section is where the artifact's credibility actually lives.
4. **In real portfolios:** the strongest "projects" sections on senior CVs are one decision, one number, and one failure, linked. The weakest are six repo names. Same bytes, opposite messages.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

- **Simplest workable approach:** one project, four artifacts (system, decision log, measured result, failure analysis), customer-stamped.
- **Real alternative:** many small artifacts (breadth) — useful early-transition, but it optimizes for *mentioning*, and FDE interviews probe depth: they will find the bottom of every artifact you bring. Depth-first once you can.
- **Failure modes:** *artifact drift* (the write-up says X, the repo does Y — sync them or date-stamp the divergence); *orphaned evidence* (claims with no links — a number without a source is decoration); *certificate leakage* (a course appears as an artifact — move it to scaffolding inside an audit row or cut it). Each: symptom (a claim you can't hand over), fix (link or delete), prevention (the stranger-in-lamplight read-through before shipping the portfolio).

### 🏝️ Location 4 — Demonstrate systems thinking and evaluation

#### 🎭 Journal Scene — The Wake, Not the Figurehead

Quest three: the well rope — logged *after* Pokkà carried the command card to the square*. Your lacquer, Franky, is the **figurehead**. Franky opens his mouth. Closes it. Then, grudgingly, with dawning respect: "…The *placement* is load-bearing. SUPER. I'm lacquering the *card's holder* next."

"And *evaluation*," Nami adds, charting, "is checking the wake every week, not photographing the figurehead. Same reading next Sunday — that's the rule now."

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"A ship has a figurehead — shiny, carved, everybody looks at it! And a wake — the water trail *behind* the ship that shows where you're *actually going* and how fast! Tourists judge the figurehead. Navigators judge the **wake**. The shiny part of a system (the UI, the demo, the table! even the LACQUER, Franky) is the figurehead. The wake is what the system *does over time* — quests appearing, villagers changing habits. Don't ask 'is it pretty?' Ask '**what trail is it leaving?**'"

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** a wake is a single visible trace; a system leaves *many* traces, and they can disagree (usage up, outcomes flat — which is true?). Reading systems means choosing *which* trace is the real motion, and the choice is a design act. The exact technical distinction: **systems thinking means modeling the whole — inputs, actors, the tool, the environment, and the feedback loops between them — so you predict where a change propagates; evaluation means defining, in advance, which observable trace constitutes 'working,' and measuring that trace on a schedule. The figurehead is any element visible but causally idle; the wake is the causal trace you elected to trust.**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

A system model you can hold in one table (this *is* the whole discipline):

| Element | Dawn Island quest system | The question it owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Villagers with problems; the command card | What enters, from whom? |
| Actors | Villager (logs), crew (triaages), maintenance crew (fixes) | Who does what, unprompted? |
| Tool | Sunny Desk: `add`, `list`, the JSON file | What transforms the input? |
| Environment | The counter's placement; rain; the old shouting habit; Pokkà's runs | What helps/hurts around the tool? |
| Feedback loops | Weekly instrument read (Nami); logged→fixed quality pair | What tells us it's working — and who's watching that? |

**Evaluation** then attaches to rows, not vibes: for each loop, a trace, a reading schedule, a threshold for action. Nami's standing rule — *same reading every Sunday* — is an evaluation design; "it feels adopted" is not. And the systems question that earns its keep: **"if I change X, what else moves?"** (Move the card from the counter to the crew's tent: input rate collapses. The card *was* the distribution system — the wake told us.)

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️ (advanced):** two advanced lenses. **Feedback delay:** adoption traces move weeks after tool changes — evaluating the day after a change measures yesterday's system, so hold the reading schedule sacred even (especially) when impatient. **Second-order effects:** every successful loop spawns new behaviors — quests being logged *for* villagers to hit targets (Day 1's Goodhart pair), or the maintenance crew ignoring a now-invisible queue. A senior systems review lists the top three *likely new behaviors* a change triggers, and pre-assigns each a trace. That list is what "systems thinking" looks like on paper.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Take Robin's observation apart — why did the *card's placement* out-perform the *lacquered table*?

1. **Causal path trace.** Quest 3 exists because: card at counter → Pokkà sees it → card travels to square → villager reads exact phrase → command runs. Six links, each necessary. Lacquer sits on *zero* causal paths to quest creation. **Trace every artifact to a behavior; artifacts on no path are decoration** (allowed — but priced and named as such).
2. **The loop that made it a system:** villager logs → crew triages Sunday → fixes happen → villagers *see writing working* → next villager logs. The last arrow is the adoption engine, and *no one designed it*; the Sunday read (Nami) is what makes it visible before it either strengthens or dies. Evaluation is the system's nervous system.
3. **Removal test, again, formally:** remove the tool → quests stop (necessary). Remove the card → quests mostly stop (the distribution *was* the feature). Remove the Sunday read → quests continue, *learning* stops — the silent failure. Necessary-but-invisible elements are where systems die quietly.
4. **Real-world shape of this:** field teams discovering "the iPad mount at the nurse's station, not the app, drove adoption" — the pattern has a name in the trade, *fitting the workflow's surface*. When your metric moves for reasons unrelated to your code, you've met the environment row of the table. Write it down; it's usually the biggest finding of the engagement.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

- **Simplest workable approach:** the five-row system table + one scheduled instrument reading + one quality pair (from Day 1: logged → actually fixed).
- **Real alternative:** instrument-heavy evaluation (event tracking, funnels) — earns its cost at scale, suffocates a village. Rule of thumb: **the instrument should be one notch more sophisticated than the decision it serves.**
- **Failure modes:** *figurehead bias* (polishing visible elements on no causal path — symptom: beautiful output, flat instrument; fix: trace-to-behavior audit); *reading theater* (readings happen, no thresholds, no action — fix: each reading names a decision it can change); *single-trace trust* (one number, no quality pair — Day 1's Goodhart warning, now structural).

#### ⚓ The Crew Debate — "What *is* the system — the tool, or the village?"

- **FRANKY:** "The tool. Code is what we ship."
- **ROBIN:** "The tool plus the card plus the Sunday reading. Remove any; the number dies. That's the system."
- **JINBE (the calm):** "And plus the habit we are slowly replacing — shouting still handles six quests a day. We are not the system yet; we are a *layer* on it. Design for coexistence, not conquest."
- **NAMI:** "Layer, then. But the Sunday reading decides when the layer becomes the system."

**Resolution:** the system = tool + distribution (card) + evaluation (Sunday read + quality pair), coexisting with the shouting layer until the instrument says otherwise. **The trade-off that remains:** coexistence means double-handling (some quests shouted *and* logged) — a real, priced cost of patience, watched via the quality pair.

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

The JD triage, five steps (Nami's three columns, operationalized):

1. **Pull the fight:** the summary + first two bullets, verbatim. ("Ship AI systems into enterprise fleets. Run discovery with customers.") That's the job.
2. **Split the column:** each requirement → *mandatory* (restated in the fight, or appears twice) or *nice-to-have* (once, adjective-heavy). 
3. **Rank by evidence, not confidence:** for each mandatory item your audit says *carry* (has artifact) / *thin* (beliefs only) / *gap* (nothing). 
4. **Mint closing artifacts only for gaps under mandatory items** — the audit's method, aimed. Nice-to-haves get nothing until mandatories are covered.
5. **Write the 90-day prep plan as artifacts, not courses:** "decision log for one RAG prototype" beats "RAG course, completed."

Sanity check at the end: if your prep plan would satisfy *any* FDE posting, it satisfies *no* posting — generic preparation is Diego's cart with extra steps.

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️ (advanced):** the JD is also a *negotiation document*. Requirements are opening positions; evidence is counter-currency: a shipped system with a decision log routinely beats two years of a listed nice-to-have. Second advanced read: the JD tells you the **interview's shape** — "50% travel + enterprise fleets" means scenario questions about discovery under constraints, not LeetCode finals; "build the platform" means architecture depth. Prepare answers in the shape the fight predicts. And postings that name *no* fight (only columns) describe a role the company itself hasn't figured out — an FDE candidate fact worth its weight in tangerines.

### 🏥 Chopper's Clinic (Top Mistakes)

1. **Résumé-driven inventory** *(beginner)* — **Symptom:** audit lists technologies, no artifacts; interviewers probe one row and find nothing behind it. **Diagnosis:** auditing by consumption, not delivery. **Fix:** re-run the audit with the evidence bar (artifact per strength); demote or delete rows that fail it. **Prevention test:** every row survives "show me" with a link.
2. **The tutorial loop** *(beginner)* — **Symptom:** months of courses, zero repos; each course ends and the knowledge evaporates. **Diagnosis:** no closing artifact ever minted; certificates as destinations. **Fix:** pick *one* gap under a mandatory JD item; build its artifact this week; log the decision. **Prevention test:** for any study hour, name the artifact it feeds.
3. **Sharpening with your own guesses** *(any level)* — **Symptom:** the restatement is precise and wrong; you build the wrong door, exactly. **Diagnosis:** ambiguity "reduced" by the engineer's imagination instead of the requester's answer. **Fix:** the impact question, answered in the requester's words, confirmed back. **Prevention test:** the requester signs the restatement (a nod counts).
4. **Generic preparation** *(production-level, career-wise)* — **Symptom:** prepared everywhere, matched nowhere; trios of JDs unread. **Diagnosis:** treating the requirement column as a universal syllabus. **Fix:** three-notice triage, monthly; emphasis follows the fights. **Prevention test:** your prep plan would *fail* at least one FDE posting — specificity is the point.

### The code (complete changed units — full files live in the repo, byte-identical)

**`storage.py` — the changed public interface** (the rest of the file is unchanged from Day 1; the format docstring gains the compatibility note):

```python
    def add_quest(self, title: str, requester: str, impact: str = "") -> dict:
        """Add one quest and return it as a new dict.

        ``impact`` is an optional plain-language note: what breaks, for
        whom, if this quest is never done. It exists because "door thing
        again" cannot be prioritized -- an honest impact note can (Day 2:
        ambiguity reduction).

        Raises ValueError for a blank title or requester: a quest nobody
        asked for, or nobody can name, cannot be prioritized or closed.
        """
        title = title.strip()
        requester = requester.strip()
        impact = impact.strip()
        if not title:
            raise ValueError("quest title must not be empty")
        if not requester:
            raise ValueError("requester must not be empty")
        quest = {
            "id": self._next_id,
            "title": title,
            "requester": requester,
            "impact": impact,
            "status": "open",
            "created_at": _utc_now_iso(),
        }
        self._quests.append(quest)
        self._next_id += 1
        self._save()
        return dict(quest)  # a copy: callers cannot mutate the store
```

### Common implementation bugs (Chopper's post-build notes)

1. **Making the new key required in the reader** (`quest["impact"]`) — crashes on every Day-1 file on first run; the tolerant `.get("impact", "")` is the whole compatibility story (and its test is the guardrail).
2. **Bumping the store version to 2 "for cleanliness"** — forces a migration for zero semantic change; additive tolerance exists precisely so you *don't*.
3. **Forgetting to `strip()` the impact** — whitespace-only impacts defeat the "echo when non-empty" check and print ghost lines.
4. **Validating impact length/content in code** — the discipline of a good impact sentence is a *docs and culture* problem (the card, the help text); code that guesses sentence quality becomes an adversary. Hold the tool honest, keep it simple.

### 🔑 Answer Key (Marine Quiz)

1. A dated artifact (repo, incident, metric) / a closing artifact — a thing you can show, not a course that "covers" it.
2. Referent, Impact, Horizon; keystone: *"What happens if it's never fixed — for whom?"* (forces a story with referents, victims, and a clock).
3. Working system, decision log, measured result, honest failure analysis. The decision log — it must record rejected alternatives *with reasons at the time*, which takes months of accumulated honesty to counterfeit.
4. The figurehead — visible but causally idle. Test: trace-to-behavior (can you draw a causal path from this element to the instrument's trace? If no path, decoration).
5. Generic preparation (Diego's cart with extra steps). Fixes: the three-notice triage (fight vs. column) and the audit join — prep plan mints artifacts only for gaps under mandatory items.
6. (a) Conflict — two stakeholders want different things: surface it, don't average it. (b) Strategic vagueness — someone benefits from fog: write the restatement and ask for confirmation (fog becomes commitment). (c) Ignorance the requester is ashamed of: ask the impact question in private, in their vocabulary. Each was a discovery, per the sidebar.
7. The figurehead/wake lens (systems + evaluation). Check 1: which trace is "usage" — is the increase the *right* actors (target users) or crew/demos? Check 2: the quality pair — logged→fixed; usage without outcome movement usually means the workflow's *outcome* step isn't wired (or the metric rewards idle activity).
8. Fight = summary + first bullets (verbatim); column split (mandatory = restated-in-fight or twice); audit join (carry/thin/gap); first artifact = closing artifact for a **gap under a mandatory item** — e.g., if discovery-with-customers is mandatory and your audit says thin, the artifact is one engagement brief + its case study.
9. Because the change was additive (optional key) with a tolerant reader — old files load unchanged, so no migration was owed; D2-01's rule: **additive keys + tolerant readers are backward compatible; removing/renaming keys forces version bumps + migrations** — the field-engineering discipline that keeps deployed systems upgradable without downtime windows.
