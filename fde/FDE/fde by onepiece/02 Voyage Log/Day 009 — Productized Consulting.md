### ⚓ Morning — Setting Sail: The Hook

Foosha Island's Mayor Bogg is not a villain. He is worse for vendors: a *good* customer with a *terrible* first question. He spreads the satchel on Makino's counter — mainland contracts, a competitor's brochure ("THE SAME MACHINE, HALF THE PRICE, UNLIMITED SUPPORT FOREVER!"), and a hand-drawn map of Foosha's market square — and says, in perfect innocence, the sentence Nami has been dreading since the dock:

"Ten days. Fixed price. Everything your village got — plus support forever. Your machine, your crew, your everything. Here—" he produces a fountain pen, "—I sign *today*."

Nami doesn't touch the pen. She pours tea instead, and asks the question that begins the day: "Mayor Bogg — who keeps Foosha's records today, and may we speak to them?"

The mayor blinks. "Records? Why, the harbor office has… some ledgers. And the market master keeps his own book, I believe. And the ferry clerk—" He falters, because he is discovering, live, that he does not actually know.

"And *that*," Nami says, kindly, "is why I'm not signing your sentence. You've offered to buy a *finished thing*. What we can honestly sell you is a *beginning* — ten days of our method, your homework listed, your unknowns priced, your forever explicitly *not* included." She flips a fresh sheet. "Give me one day, Mayor, and I'll show you the difference between buying a machine, hiring a crew, and something better than both."

Jinbe, to the crew, under his breath: "Watch the navigator closely today. Everything we built these eight days is about to become a *page*."

> **📖 What today is really about.** The syllabus's meaning line, verbatim: *"Productized consulting packages repeatable delivery knowledge into bounded offerings without hiding customer-specific uncertainty."* Five skills turn eight days of one-village craft into something a second village can buy — a repeatable outcome, a standardized kit, explicit exclusions and change control, reused tested components, and a channel that feeds repeated needs back into the product. The failure mode is Mayor Bogg's sentence and half the industry's contracts: *"promising fixed delivery effort while access, source quality, and stakeholder availability remain unknown."*

### 🏝️ Location 1 — Define a repeatable outcome

#### 🎭 Journal Scene

Nami's one-day deadline produces, by lamplight, a single page. She reads it to Mayor Bogg slowly, like a sea chart being unrolled:

> **THE VILLAGE COUNTER — deployed in ten days.**
> **Outcome (what you will have on day ten):** your villagers report problems at a counter; the requests are written, countable, and reconciled with your maintenance crew's records; your operator and a deputy can run, back up, diagnose, and recover the logbook *unaided* — proven by a witnessed rehearsal.
> **Proof it repeats:** this exact page produced Dawn Island's counter. Every artifact below exists in two copies — one running there, one shipped to you.

Bogg taps the page: "And *this* is what I'm buying? Not the machine?"

"The machine is a plank," Nami says. "You're buying the *shipyard* — the method that turned an empty beach into a working harbor office in eight days, packaged so it can happen again on *your* beach. The ten days aren't a guess. They're the sum of steps that already happened once, with names, artifacts, and tests."

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"A cook can sell you ONE stew — delicious, gone in a minute! Or the cook can sell you *the recipe plus the shopping list plus the lesson* — stew forever, in your kitchen, tasting like YOUR stove! Productized consulting is the second thing! But here's the trick, and it's the whole trick: the recipe has to be TRUE. It has to be the recipe that *actually cooked the stew you tasted* — with the steps, the exact ingredients, and honest notes like 'your stove might run hotter; check at step four.' A recipe written from memory after one lucky stew is a rumor with garnish!"

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** a recipe's ingredients behave identically in every kitchen; customers do not — Foosha's records, stakeholders, and rain are *unknowns*, which is why the page's honesty lives in its *boundaries* (the homework list, the exclusions), not just its steps. The exact technical distinction: **a repeatable outcome is a deliverable state defined precisely enough to verify the same way on every engagement ("operator and deputy run, back up, diagnose, and recover unaided — proven by witnessed rehearsal"), produced by a documented method whose artifacts are standardized across deliveries. It differs from bespoke consulting (no defined outcome) and from a product sale (no delivery method): productization is the packaging of *method* around a component, with uncertainty declared rather than absorbed.**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

The outcome sentence has four load-bearing parts (each maps to an artifact that already exists from Days 1–8):

| Outcome component | Dawn Island proof artifact |
| --- | --- |
| *What the customer can DO on day ten* (the state, not the features) | villagers log; crew reconciles; operator runs unaided |
| *How it's verified* (the same test every time) | the UAT session sheet's criteria + the silence test |
| *What it's built from* (the kit) | Sunny Desk v0.1.8 + six signed artifacts |
| *What it's honest about* (the boundary) | the exclusions page (Loop 3) |

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️:** the deep economic point: a repeatable outcome is what makes delivery *sellable at a fixed price honestly*. Bespoke consulting absorbs unknowns inside a margin of hope; a productized offering moves the unknowns *out of the promise and into the homework list* — the customer's inputs become contractual, which is why the honest fixed price is possible at all. This is also why productization is earned, not declared: you can only package the method after it has produced the outcome **more than once**, with artifacts that survived contact with a real customer. Dawn Island is the first run; Foosha is the packaging's first *honest test* — and the syllabus's mastery line is precise about what repeat means: *consistent artifacts, exceptions explicit.*

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Dissect Nami's page as a packaging of the previous eight days:

1. **Every sentence cites a shipped artifact:** "reconciled with your crew's records" = SHED_LINK; "run, back up, diagnose, recover unaided" = RUNBOOK v3 + the doctor; "proven by witnessed rehearsal" = the silence test. The page is *new*; nothing in it is. That is the productization signature — the offering's cost of creation was already paid.
2. **What the page deliberately omits:** features. No mention of `stats`'s top-tags, no `show`, no implementation detail — the outcome state is the product; the mechanism is delivery detail. (Customers buy Tuesday, not architecture.)
3. **The "proof it repeats" line is the trust anchor:** a stranger can verify the claim by inspecting two artifact sets side by side. Remove that line and the page is a brochure; with it, the page is a *citation*.
4. **Removal test:** delete the outcome sentence and keep the features — Mayor Bogg is back to "machine, half price, forever." The outcome sentence is the only thing on the page that isn't a commodity.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

- **Simplest workable approach:** one page — outcome sentence, proof-of-repetition line, kit list, homework list, exclusions, change control, escalation. (Everything Nami writes today lands in `OFFERING.md`.)
- **Real alternative:** sell the machine (Franky's instinct) — product-only; the customer inherits Day-2 alone. It closes faster and dies slower, in exactly the way that generates the industry's horror stories.
- **Trade-offs:** productization constrains the crew (Foosha gets the *method*, not Dawn Island's improvisations — the method must be *good enough to bind*); the page becomes a promise with a signature under it.
- **Failure modes:** *the packaged fantasy* (a page describing what happened in someone's memory, not what the artifacts show — fix: every claim cites a shipped artifact); *the absorption trap* (unknowns quietly folded into the fixed price — Loop 3's whole subject); *the outcome drift* (each delivery "slightly better" until nothing repeats — fix: change control, Loop 3).

#### ⚓ The Crew Debate — "Is one delivery enough to sell the method?"

- **FRANKY:** "One is a prototype! Ship it to Foosha and we're a PRODUCT COMPANY, bro!"
- **ROBIN:** "One is a data point wearing a success story. The page sells the *method*; the method has run once. The honesty lives in saying so."
- **JINBE (the calm):** "Which is why the page sells a *bounded engagement*, not a franchise. Foosha is the second run. If its artifacts match ours — *then* the method is real. The mayor is not buying certainty; he is buying a disciplined first date with it."

### 🏝️ Location 2 — Standardize discovery inputs and delivery artifacts

#### 🎭 Journal Scene

Page two of Nami's offer is the one Mayor Bogg signs *without reading* — so she makes him read it. Two columns:

> **THE KIT — what the crew brings (no extra charge, non-negotiable):** the counter software, tested (23 cases) · the runbook v3 with four rehearsed playbooks · the responsibility matrix, pre-drafted for your signatures · the shed-link treaty template · the acceptance-session sheet · the demo data, seeded in one command · the training morning.
>
> **YOUR HOMEWORK — what Foosha owes before day one (the ten days assume these exist):** a named sponsor (you) with authority to sign · one counter machine · a named operator + deputy (humans, willing) · your records-keeper found and *introduced* (we cannot integrate a ledger nobody will admit to) · your shed crew's consent to sign the treaty · one hour of the mayor's time on days 1, 5, and 10.

Bogg reads the homework column twice. "This is… a list of my own paperwork."

"It's the list of things that, unknown, would break the ten days," Nami says. "Your competitor's brochure promises *forever support* and says nothing about your harbor clerk. I promise ten days and tell you exactly which of *your* doors must be open. Fixed price is only honest when the inputs are fixed too."

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"Every recipe starts with 'YOU WILL NEED:' — that's the homework column! The cook brings the method; YOU bring the kitchen, the stove, and the fish! If you promise 'stew in one hour' without asking about the stove first, YOU'RE the one who gets burned! Same with adventure: we bring the ship, the charts, the drills — Foosha brings the harbor, the harbor master, and a mayor who answers on days one, five, and ten! The list isn't rude. The list is the *only reason the promise can be true!*"

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** recipes assume kitchens exist; engagement inputs include *human availability and political access* — the items most likely to be unknown precisely because nobody wants to admit their harbor clerk is a mystery. The exact technical distinction: **standardization has two halves: delivery artifacts (what every engagement produces — brief, matrix, treaty, session sheet, runbook: the same species, stamped per customer) and discovery inputs (what every customer must supply before day one — sponsor, operator, records access, consents). The offering is honest when the inputs are listed as *conditions*, the artifacts are named as *deliverables*, and the ten-day timeline is stated as a conditional: "ten days *given* these inputs; slippage is change-controlled, not absorbed."**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

| | The kit (we bring) | The homework (you owe) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | standardized delivery artifacts | conditions of the timeline |
| Foosha instance | software + runbook + matrix template + session sheet + demo seed | sponsor, machine, operator+deputy, records-keeper introduction, shed consent, mayoral hours |
| If missing at day one | built *into* the price of the first change (change control) | the clock pauses — visibly, in writing (Loop 3) |

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️:** the Applied Exercise's audit is this discipline at its purest — a **data-readiness audit** is *only* input-checking: access exists? sources named? quality measurable? stakeholders reachable? The audit sells because it is fixed-scope *about the unknowns themselves*: its output is a written map of what the customer's data actually is, and its honesty requirement is that it discovers rather than assumes. The reason audits productize beautifully (and "implementations" rarely do) is that an audit's inputs are *always available* — asking questions requires nothing but an appointment. When you design yours (tonight's assignment), standardize the questionnaire first: the same six questions, asked the same way, every time — that's what makes report #7 comparable to report #1.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Dissect one homework row — "your records-keeper found and introduced" — as a priced unknown:

1. **Where it came from:** the wrecking-ball morning (Day 7). Foosha's harbor office has "some ledgers" the *mayor cannot name* — the exact condition that produced Franky's double-write. The homework row is that incident, converted into a contract clause.
2. **Why it's a *condition* and not a task:** the crew cannot find Foosha's clerk for them — access to stakeholders is sovereign, like the data. The ten-day clock's honesty depends on the difference: conditions pause the clock; tasks consume it.
3. **What standardization bought:** because every Dawn Island artifact exists, the Foosha kit is *assembled*, not authored — Nami's packaging day cost one day, not eight. That ratio (package once, deliver many) is the entire economics of productized consulting.
4. **Removal test:** delete the homework column and the ten-day promise silently absorbs Foosha's unknowns — the syllabus's failure mode, executed by omission. The homework list is the page's insurance policy for the *seller*.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

- **Simplest workable approach:** the two-column page (kit/homework), each row traceable to a Day-1–8 artifact or incident.
- **Real alternative:** the unlimited-support brochure — sells beautifully, delivers litigation.
- **Trade-offs:** the homework list costs deals (some mayors walk) — and the ones who walk were the fixed-price horror stories, pre-emptively declined; the kit must be *maintained* (v0.1.8 ships inside the offering — version drift is change-controlled).
- **Failure modes:** *the invisible homework* (inputs assumed, absorbed, resented) → the column, signed; *kit drift* (each delivery's kit diverges) → the kit is versioned like code (it *is* code); *questionnaire improvisation* (discovery asks different questions per customer) → standardized discovery inputs, per the syllabus — the same questions, every time.

#### ⚓ The Crew Debate — "Should the demo seed Foosha's real quests?"

- **FRANKY:** "One command, bro! Their data pre-loaded, day one—"
- **USOPP:** "Their data?! We don't KNOW their data! That's the whole homework column!"
- **NAMI:** "The demo seeds the *standard three* — synthetic, shaped like reality, honest about being training wheels. Their real quests enter through discovery, not through me guessing at their harbor."
- **JINBE (the calm):** "The command's refusal is the lesson, shipwright: it seeds fresh stores *only*. An engagement repeats; it never presumes."

### 🏝️ Location 3 — State exclusions and change control

#### 🎭 Journal Scene

Page three is short, and Nami makes Mayor Bogg read it *aloud* — a trick she learned from watching the Mayor of Dawn sign the prototype clause.

> **WHAT THIS PRICE DOES NOT INCLUDE:** forever-operations (Day 2 is *taught and transferred* — the roster and runbook become yours; our on-call is two festival-weeks, lapsing by its own condition). Unbounded custom (new features are scoped as changes, priced as changes). Unknowns absorbed (access, source quality, stakeholder availability — priced as discovered, never as hoped). Silence as consent (any scope change is written, priced, and initialed — Day 3's prototype clause, wearing a contract).

Bogg reads "forever" twice. "Every brochure I have ever signed promised forever."

"Every brochure you signed was a lawsuit on a delay," Jinbe says pleasantly. "The word *forever* is the most expensive word in the sea. We sell you a *beginning* with a trained crew on shore — and we put our own absence in writing, because it is certain. Exclusions are not us refusing work, Mayor. Exclusions are us telling you, in advance, where the truth ends."

And then Nami does the thing the crew will talk about for islands: she hands Bogg the pen for *her* page. "You sign my exclusions, or there's no engagement. If my honesty frightens you, my deadlines should too."

He signs. The harbor clerk, produced at last by a mayor who now understands why the question mattered, is introduced to the crew by name.

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"'Forever' is the word that eats ships! Every promise needs its *edges* written down — where the food ends, where the sea begins! Exclusions are the EDGES of the deal! And change control is the ship's LAW for new cargo: everything new gets weighed, priced, and *signed for* before it boards! No stowaways! The brochures that promise everything board everything — and sink of cargo! Ours says NO on paper, kindly, in advance — so YES still means something!"

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** ship cargo is weighed in kilos; scope is weighed in *unknowns* — and the heaviest stowaways are the ones nobody knows to declare ("we assumed the harbor clerk existed"). The exact technical distinction: **exclusions define the boundary of the promise (operations-transfer yes, forever-operations no; bounded custom yes, unbounded custom no); change control defines the *process at the boundary* — any request beyond the offered scope becomes a written, priced, separately-approved change, never an absorbed expectation. Together they protect both sides: the customer learns what they are and are not buying (before the dispute), and the deliverer's fixed price stays honest (the syllabus's failure mode is fixed effort *plus unknown inputs* — exclusions and homework move the unknowns into the light before the signature).**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

| Boundary tool | The sentence it writes | The failure it prevents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exclusions** | "This price does not include forever-operations / unbounded custom / absorbed unknowns" | The brochure's silent everything |
| **Change control** | "New scope = written, priced, initialed — never absorbed" | Scope creep disguised as friendship |
| **Conditions** (homework) | "Ten days *given* these inputs; slippage pauses the clock, visibly" | The fixed-price death march |
| **The prototype clause** (inherited) | "An impressive demo transfers nothing" | Day-3's trap, contractually |

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️:** the AI-era exclusion that belongs on every modern offering, learned early: **model behavior is a dependency** (Day 8's register) — so the honest page excludes "answer quality guarantees" for any AI-assisted component and replaces them with *measured* quality commitments (evaluation harnesses, thresholds — the truth court). The village-scale version already exists in miniature: the counter's objectives are checkable rather than adjectival. Productized offerings in the AI age are, above all, *boundary documents* — and the crew is writing theirs two islands before the industry finishes learning why.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Dissect the exclusion page's power move — Bogg signs *her* page:

1. **The reversal's mechanics:** vendors normally sign the customer's paper. By requiring her exclusions as the signing surface, Nami makes honesty the *governing document* — the brochure's "forever" becomes legally subordinate to the page's edges. Contracts follow the signature order; she made honesty first.
2. **What each exclusion protects, traceably:** "no forever-operations" protects the crew's Day-3 victory (the volunteer-everything trap); "unknowns priced as discovered" protects the ten-day promise from Day-7's morning; "no silence-as-consent" protects Makino's Sunday from Bogg's future ideas. Each clause is a past battle, ratified.
3. **The introduction of the harbor clerk** — the scene's quiet payoff: the exclusions conversation *produced* the discovery input. Boundaries, honestly stated, are themselves discovery instruments: a customer who reads "we need your records-keeper introduced" goes and finds them.
4. **Removal test:** strike the exclusions and keep the fixed price — the engagement is now a bet on Foosha's unknowns, and the syllabus's failure mode is no longer dramatized; it is *scheduled*.

#### 🏗️ Architecture, Trade-offs & Failure Modes

- **Simplest workable approach:** the four boundary tools on one page, signed *before* the work starts, inherited artifacts referenced by name.
- **Real alternative:** "we'll be flexible" — flexibility is a lovely word for absorbing unknowns until the fixed price and the truth collide.
- **Trade-offs:** the page costs deals and costs the crew improvisation freedom (a signed method binds the crew too — Franky cannot fourteen-arrow Foosha); exclusions must be *maintained* as the kit evolves.
- **Failure modes:** *exclusion rot* (the page says no to things no longer offered) → versioned with the kit; *the polite absorption* (verbal exceptions to written exclusions) → the page is the record; *change-control theater* (changes written but never priced) → every change carries a number and a signature.

#### ⚓ The Crew Debate — "Two festival-weeks of on-call — is that 'forever' in disguise?"

- **MAYOR BOGG:** "It says lapsing! Lapsing is forever with better manners!" 
- **NAMI:** "It says the *condition* that lapses it — written, dated, and mine to enforce. Forever has no condition. This has one."
- **JINBE (the calm):** "And the condition is testable, Mayor: two weeks, then your roster answers. That is the difference between a promise and a leash — the leash is on us, on paper, with dates."

### 🏝️ Location 4 — Reuse tested components

#### 🎭 Journal Scene

Franky's redemption day. He stands before the Foosha kit — one crate, packed by hand — and inventories it like a proud parent: the counter software (v0.1.8, 26 tests green), the runbook (four playbooks, rehearsed), the matrix template, the treaty template, the session sheet, and a folded card with a single command on it.

"Brothers," he says, voice cracking slightly under the mustache, "do you know what is NOT in this crate? A single new thing. Eight days of Dawn Island, and Foosha gets — *the same crate*. The tests that guard Makino's logbook will guard Foosha's. The playbook that caught our corrupt file catches theirs. I built ONE thing—" he catches himself, grinning, "—we built one thing *eight times stronger*, and now it ships *twice*."

The one new thing is tonight's `demo` command — and even that is mostly *old*: it reuses the store, the idempotency discipline from the treaty, and the three standard quests. It seeds a fresh village file in one command and — the detail Franky points to twice — **refuses a non-empty target**: *"demo seeds only FRESH stores (nothing was written)."* An engagement repeats; it never double-writes.

Chief T., who has wandered over to see what all the packing is about, reads the card. "So Foosha's logbook starts with… the same three problems mine did?"

"Training wheels," Makino says. "Synthetic, shaped like reality. Their real quests come through discovery — not through us guessing at their harbor." Chief T. nods slowly. "Good. Nobody should start a logbook with someone else's well rope in it."

#### 📖 Feynman Notes

**🍖 ELI5 — Luffy's version:**

"You know why pirate ships all look the SAME underneath? Because a hull that survived one storm is a *tested hull*! You don't carve a new hull per voyage — you copy the hull, and you spend your energy on the NEW stuff — the flags, the cargo, the adventure! Reusing tested components is hull-thinking: the counter, the runbook, the matrix, the treaty — *survived hulls*! Foosha gets the same hull and paints her OWN flags! And the demo command? It's the hull's keel-laying ceremony: one command, same starting shape, and — this is the beautiful part — it REFUSES to stamp a keel on a ship that already has one!"

> **⚠️ Where the analogy breaks:** a copied hull is identical in every plank; a reused *component* gets configured per customer (the treaty signs different names, the matrix assigns different people) — reuse means *same tested core, declared configuration*, not identical instance. And hulls don't version; the kit does — Foosha's crate carries v0.1.8, and the difference between v0.1.7 and v0.1.8 is change-controlled (Loop 3). The exact technical distinction: **reuse means each delivery consumes the same tested, versioned components (code, playbooks, templates, procedures) rather than rebuilding per customer — with configuration (names, paths, terms) declared per engagement and the component's version recorded, so every delivery is comparable and every defect fixed once fixes all.**

**⚔️ STANDARD — Engineer's version:**

| Component | Version | Flexes per customer? | Test coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Counter software | v0.1.8 (26 tests) | store path + demo seed only | executed suite |
| Runbook | v3 (4 playbooks) | names/phones in the roster | rehearsed (silence test) |
| Matrix + treaty templates | as signed on Dawn | every name, every row | re-signed per engagement (Day 3/7 rules) |
| Session sheet | Day-6 format | criteria per workflow | pre-signed per UAT |
| Demo seed | 3 standard quests | *never* — synthetic by design | refusal test |

**Skypiea sidebar ☠️:** the strategic sentence underneath the crate: **the tested component is the FDE's compounding asset.** Bespoke consultants start from zero each engagement; productized ones start from *version N* — every engagement's findings feed the next version (Loop 5), and the cost curve bends while the competitor's stays flat. The risk it carries: components ossify — what protected Dawn Island may misfit a harbor with different tides, and the reuse instinct can become a refusal to hear the new customer. The countermeasure is Day 5's discipline wearing a shipping label: every engagement files its divergences (the delta ledger), and *the kit's version changes only through change control* — reuse is a treaty with the customer, not a cage for the method.

#### 🔬 Reverse-Engineering Notes

Dissect `demo`'s refusal as a reuse safeguard:

1. **The failure it prevents:** Franky's Day-7 wrecking ball, productized — an auto-seeder that appends "the standard three" to an existing store would double-write any village that already started logging. The refusal encodes the treaty's first rule (fresh stores only) at the *component* level.
2. **Why refusal beats overwrite-or-merge:** merging "standard" quests into real ones is ambiguity (which rope is theirs?); overwriting destroys; refusing preserves. The component's design says: *the customer's reality outranks the demo's convenience* — the same sovereignty principle from Day 7, now in a seed command.
3. **What the three standard quests are:** synthetic, shape-true (a natural disaster, an infrastructure gap, a duplicate-tagged confusion) — they exercise the exact paths the training covers, *without* pretending to be Foosha's data. Training wheels, honestly labeled.

### 🏥 Chopper's Clinic (Top Mistakes)

1. **The everything brochure** *(the syllabus's failure mode)* — **Symptom:** "ten days, fixed price, forever support"; delivery drowns in Foosha's unknowns. **Diagnosis:** fixed effort promised while access, quality, and stakeholders are unknown. **Fix:** the homework column + exclusions + change control, signed before work. **Prevention test:** your page names every input the timeline depends on — and who owes each.
2. **The packaged fantasy** *(beginner)* — **Symptom:** the offering page describes a delivery that happened *once, mostly, from memory*. **Diagnosis:** packaging from memory instead of from artifacts. **Fix:** every claim cites a shipped artifact (Nami's page cites seven). **Prevention test:** a stranger can verify the "proof it repeats" line from the repo.
3. **The absorbing habit** *(any level)* — **Symptom:** "sure, we can add that" — spoken warmly, priced never. **Diagnosis:** no change control; scope creep as customer service. **Fix:** new scope = written, priced, initialed — the kindest no is a priced yes. **Prevention test:** the last three scope changes have numbers and signatures.
4. **The ossified kit** *(practitioner)* — **Symptom:** every delivery uses the same crate; customers adapt to the tool instead of the reverse. **Diagnosis:** reuse without the feed — divergences never filed. **Fix:** the delta ledger's engagement entries + change-controlled kit versions. **Prevention test:** the kit's version history names the customer signal that drove each change.

### 🎯 Zoro's Training Ground (Practice)

Four problems + one trap. **Today's invariant:** *no page without proof-of-repetition; no promise without its exclusions; no pattern without a filing.*

1. **(easy)** Write your own repeatable-outcome sentence for any service you've delivered twice (moving help, tutoring, a script you've deployed for two friends) — state, verify, kit, boundary.
2. **(medium)** The syllabus's exercise: design the **fixed-scope data-readiness audit** — inputs (what the client owes), outputs (what they receive), timeline assumptions, acceptance criteria, escalation path. One page.
3. **(medium)** Exclusions drill: take any promise you've made recently ("I'll help with your website") and write its boundary page — what's included, what's excluded, what a change costs.
4. **(hard)** Audit a real offering (yours or a public one): find its kit/homework split, its exclusions, and the one unknown it silently absorbs. Draft the clause that fixes it.
5. **⚠️ TRAP PROBLEM — a pricing question wearing a generosity costume:** Foosha's mayor, grateful, asks: "Since you're already here — just *include* the training for our market master? No charge? You're here anyway." Diagnose using today's material: what is actually being asked, what does "free" cost, and the reply that keeps the relationship *and* the boundary.

**Hints:** (2) The audit's acceptance criteria are the deliverable — an audit without a defined report is a fishing trip. (4) The silently-absorbed unknown is usually *stakeholder availability*. (5) "You're here anyway" is the most expensive sentence in consulting.

**Approaches:** (1) Strong sentences are verifiable states, not adjectives. (2) Strong audits standardize the questionnaire (same questions, every client) and define the report before the questions. (3) The boundary page protects the *relationship* — the absorbed favor is the one that sours it. (4) Every real offering absorbs something; finding it is the exercise. (5) It asks the crew to convert a *change request* (market-master training = new stakeholder, new session, new materials) into an *absorbed expectation* — establishing "already here = free" as the engagement's precedent, which resurfaces at every future boundary; the free training also skips change control, so its scope and quality have no definition. The reply: "Gladly — as the first *change order*, priced fairly, and I'll show you exactly what it includes. You're not paying for my hours, Mayor; you're paying for the promise that stays true." (The trap: generosity that trades a boundary for a smile — and teaches the customer to ask again.)

3. **"Where does consulting end and product begin?"** — Strong: the two-harbor rule, the maintained product, change control as the seam; D-002-style filing. Weak: a philosophy essay.
4. **"Your competitor undercuts you with 'unlimited support.'"** — Strong: price what their sentence absorbs (operations forever, unknowns, support as a leash); sell the honest page; let the exclusions win the renewal. Weak: matching their promise.
5. **"What did your last customer teach your product?"** — Strong: a filed delta with evidence and the version it shipped in. Weak: "we listen to all feedback."

#### 📜 Assignment Orders (graded — the syllabus's Applied Exercise)

**Goal:** *"Design a fixed-scope data-readiness audit with inputs, outputs, timeline assumptions, acceptance criteria, and an escalation path."*

- **Scope:** one page, the five headings verbatim. **Inputs** = what the client owes before day one (access, named data owners, stakeholder hours). **Outputs** = the deliverables (the readiness report: source inventory, access matrix, quality findings, risk register — standardized so audit #7 is comparable to audit #1). **Timeline assumptions** = the conditions the clock rests on (and what pauses it). **Acceptance criteria** = how the *report itself* is verdicted (a stranger can act on it). **Escalation path** = what happens when the audit finds something scary.
- **Requirements & acceptance criteria:** every input is owed by a *named role*; the questionnaire is standardized (same questions every client); the exclusions (what the audit does NOT do — no fixes, just findings) are stated; a stranger could run audit #2 from your page.
- **Deliverables:** `readiness-audit.md`.

### 🔑 Answer Key (Marine Quiz)

1. **Bespoke consulting:** delivery shaped entirely per customer; outcome defined loosely; cost absorbed by hope. **Product sale:** a component handed over; no delivery method attached; Day 2 is the customer's problem alone. **Productized consulting:** the *method* packaged around tested components — a defined, verifiable outcome, standardized artifacts, explicit homework, exclusions, and change control; uncertainty priced, not promised away.
2. **Kit** = the standardized delivery artifacts every engagement receives (software, runbook, matrix/treaty templates, session sheet, demo seed, training). **Homework** = the conditions the timeline rests on (sponsor, machine, operator+deputy, records-keeper introduced, consents). The homework column prevents the syllabus's failure mode — fixed effort promised while access, source quality, and stakeholder availability remain unknown — by moving the unknowns into a signed conditions list.
3. **Exclusions** (what the price does not include — forever-operations, unbounded custom, absorbed unknowns), **change control** (new scope = written, priced, initialed), **conditions** (the timeline's "given these inputs" clause), and the **prototype clause** ("an impressive demo transfers nothing"). Signed before work began because contracts follow signature order — honesty must govern the paper, or the brochure does.
4. Flexes: configuration only — names, paths, terms, signed rows (treaty signatories, matrix owners, roster entries). Never: the tested core (code, playbooks, templates, the three synthetic quests). The demo's refusal protects **sovereignty**: a store that already holds a village's reality is never overwritten or merged by a convenience — the customer's data outranks the component's eagerness (Day 7's rule + Day 9's CLI).
5. The ask: convert a change request (new stakeholder, new session, new materials) into an absorbed expectation — and set the precedent that "you're here anyway = free." Cost: the boundary (every future ask resurfaces at the same precedent), the scope (undefined training has no acceptance criteria), and the change-control channel itself. The reply: "Gladly — as the first change order, priced fairly. You're not paying for my hours; you're paying for the promise that stays true."
6. Their price absorbs: (a) **operations forever** — an unbounded liability that scales with every customer they sign; (b) **the unknowns** — access, source quality, stakeholder availability, absorbed into the margin of hope; (c) **the exclusions** — which is to say, the truth. The rebuild: the same page with a homework column, a lapsing on-call condition, and change control — at which point the price rises to honesty, and the "half price" was the sound of boundaries being sold.
7. Strong acceptance criteria for the *report*: every source in the inventory is named by owner and access path; the access matrix reflects reality (verified, not declared); each quality finding carries evidence (sample, date, method); the risk register items are ranked with owners; and a stranger reading the report can act on it without the auditor present — the Mayor test, applied to a document.
8. Through change control: (1) the request is written ("star ratings on quests — for whom, deciding what?"); (2) scoped — it touches the data model (a new field), the CLI, tests, the card, Foosha's crate (one change, both instances — versioned); (3) priced — estimate + the timeline's honest extension or an explicit descope; (4) initialed by Bogg. Outcome A: approved as change order — the timeline moves, the price moves, in writing. Outcome B: declined-with-reason (post-festival candidate, filed on the delta watchlist) — which still closes the loop and teaches the customer the channel works. What never happens: absorbed with a smile.
9. Because merging "the standard three" into an existing store is the wrecking ball in miniature — it writes where a sovereign already rules, and ambiguity (which rope is theirs?) follows. The refusal compounds **Day 7** (an engagement repeats; it never double-writes — idempotency-by-refusal at the treaty's heart) and **Day 9's own boundary discipline** (the customer's reality outranks the component's convenience) — with Day 5's spirit in it: the demo is training wheels, honestly synthetic, never a presumption about anyone's harbor.
