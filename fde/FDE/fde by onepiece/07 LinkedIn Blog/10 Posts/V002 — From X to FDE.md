# From X to FDE: How Engineers, Analysts, and Consultants Actually Make the Jump

📷 [IMAGE 1: post-V002 header — alt: "FDE Series, Part 2: From X to FDE — the evidence-based transition"]

## TL;DR

- Nobody starts as a Forward Deployed Engineer from zero — everyone starts *from X*: backend, data analysis, consulting, support, even student projects. The transition is an **audit and a bridge**, not a rebirth.
- Five skills run the bridge: auditing what you already carry, cutting ambiguity, building customer-facing artifacts, thinking in systems, and reading job descriptions the way hiring managers write them.
- The trap that ends careers: **collecting certificates without evidence of delivering under customer constraints.**

## Assumptions & scope

- **Audience:** career-changers and engineers eyeing FDE roles; managers coaching them. No FDE background needed (see Part 1 for the role itself).
- **Scope:** the transition method. Interview prep specifics belong to a later post. **Math:** none — this is a method post; the derivations live in our foundations track.
- **Everything below assumes the definition from Part 1:** an FDE owns discovery → build → deploy → handover for one customer at a time and returns lessons to the product.

## 1. Audit before you shop: what you already carry

Before learning anything new, run a five-column audit on yourself:

| Column | The question | The quality bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prior role / project | Where did you actually work? | Non-engineering roles count — support and consulting teach customer truth |
| Carries over | What transfers — **with evidence?** | "Built the intake script that processed 300 tickets/week for 6 months" — not "knows Python" |
| Gap | What's missing that FDE work needs? | Three at a time, maximum. Twenty gaps = zero gaps |
| Closing artifact | What *thing you can show* closes it? | An artifact, not a course: a repo, a decision log, a measured result |
| Deliberately won't learn | What are you choosing not to close? | Named and owned — you'll partner instead |

The test for every row: **can a stranger verify it without talking to you?** If not, it's a belief, not an asset. And the won't-learn column is what makes the audit a plan instead of a wish list.

## 2. Ambiguity reduction: one question does most of the work

Vague requests ("the dashboard is slow," "the report is broken again") die to three axes — **RAR**: **Referent** (which one, specifically?), **Impact** (what happens if it's never fixed, for whom?), **Horizon** (by when?).

The keystone is the impact question: *"What happens if this is never fixed?"* You cannot answer it with an adjective. The reply arrives pre-structured: harm → affected party → deadline — everything you need to prioritize. Weak interviewees ask twenty questions; strong ones ask one that forces a story.

☠️ **Advanced note:** ambiguity has three sources needing three fixes — ignorance (ask the impact question), conflict between stakeholders (surface it; never average it into mush), and strategic vagueness (write the restatement down and ask for confirmation — fog becomes commitment).

## 3. The portfolio's four species — and why certificates aren't one

A transition portfolio needs exactly four kinds of artifact, each checkable by a stranger:

1. **A working system** — cloneable, runnable, with a README.
2. **A decision log** — what you chose, what you rejected, why, *dated at the time*. This is the artifact you can't fake quickly, which is why interviewers trust it most.
3. **A measured result** — a baseline, an instrument, a number. "Unknown baseline, here's how I'd measure" is acceptable; no measurement thinking is not.
4. **An honest failure analysis** — the section amateurs skip and panels probe hardest. A case study without failures reads as marketing.

Certificates can appear only as scaffolding *inside* an audit row ("used course X to build artifact Y") — never as the row itself.

## 4. Judge the wake, not the figurehead

When you evaluate a system — including your own side project — trace **causal paths**, not visible polish. The analogy we use in the series: the lacquered dashboard is the figurehead; the trail in the water is the wake. A beautiful output on zero causal paths to user behavior is decoration. The audit question that finds decoration: *can I draw the path from this element to a measurable behavior?*

## 5. Read the job description like the person who wrote it

A posting has two parts: the **fight** (the summary and first bullets — the actual job) and the **gym list** (the requirement column — the recruiter's de-risking wish). The five-step triage:

1. Pull the fight, verbatim.
2. Split the column: mandatory ≈ restated in the fight or listed twice; everything else is nice-to-have.
3. Join with your audit: carry / thin / gap per mandatory item.
4. Mint closing artifacts **only** for gaps under mandatory items.
5. Write the 90-day plan as artifacts, not courses.

Sanity check: if your prep plan would satisfy *any* FDE posting, it satisfies *none* of them.

## What this means for your organization

- **Hiring managers:** screen for the four artifact species, not credential mass — the engagement model makes a credential-heavy, delivery-light hire expensive fast. Ask "walk me through a decision you'd reverse today."
- **L&D / team leads:** convert course budgets into artifact budgets: every learning hour should feed a named artifact.
- **For founders:** your best first FDE is often internal — the engineer who keeps getting pulled into customer calls. An audit + one engagement usually converts them faster than any external hire.
- **Career-changers:** your "non-technical" past is an asset column — support, consulting, and analysis teach the discovery half of the role that career engineers lack.

## Practice checkpoint

Take one past project and outline its case study in five headed sections: context & users, stakeholder decisions (≥3, each with a rejected alternative), constraints, evidence (baseline honest), failure analysis (≥1 you'd let an interviewer probe). That outline *is* the transition — the writing just makes it legible.

## Glossary

- **RAR triad:** Referent, Impact, Horizon — the three axes that sharpen a vague request.
- **Closing artifact:** a checkable thing (repo, log, metric, post-mortem) that demonstrates a capability.
- **Decision log:** dated record of choices, rejected alternatives, and reasons.
- **Fight vs. gym list:** a JD's actual job vs. its requirement wishlist.
- **Figurehead vs. wake:** visible-but-causally-idle elements vs. the behavioral trail a system leaves.
