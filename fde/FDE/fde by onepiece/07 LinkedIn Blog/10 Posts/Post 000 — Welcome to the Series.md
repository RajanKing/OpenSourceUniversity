# The Forward Deployed Engineer — A Complete Series, From First Principles to Production

📷 [IMAGE 1: series-banner.png — alt: "Series banner: a compass and route crossing islands from foundations to production systems"]

**The job title is new. The job is not.** Somewhere in your company right now, someone is standing in the gap between "we bought a powerful AI product" and "our team's Tuesday actually got better." That person runs discovery with real users, builds against real constraints, deploys into a real environment, and answers for the result. This series is about that job — the **Forward Deployed Engineer** — and about every skill it stands on.

## What this series is

A complete, ordered, public map of the discipline:

- **The voyage track — 95 posts.** The entire Forward Deployed Engineer roadmap: the role and its vocabulary, programming and systems, full-stack and data engineering, LLMs, prompting, retrieval-augmented generation, vector databases, agents and tool ecosystems, integration with legacy systems, cloud and containers, security and observability, evaluation, customer discovery, ROI, and a full capstone.
- **The foundations track — 24 deep-dives.** Everything underneath: Python, the data stack, SQL, exploratory data analysis, linear algebra, probability, statistics, dimensionality reduction, every classical model from k-NN to XGBoost (with the derivations, not just the API calls), deep learning, convolutional and recurrent networks, attention, and a real-world case-study playbook.

One post per topic. Each post stands alone; together they form one resource.

## Who it's for (be skeptical — this is a real answer)

- **If you're starting out:** every post opens with a plain-language summary and closes with a glossary. Nothing is assumed that isn't declared.
- **If you're a practitioner:** we show the math. Bayes' theorem gets derived, not quoted. Gradient descent, backpropagation, PCA's eigen-problem, why TF-IDF takes a logarithm, why attention scales the dot product — proven, with worked numbers, then shown in code.
- **If you build or run a company:** every post ends with "what this means for your organization" — when to hire for this role, what it should cost, what to measure, and the honest failure modes that turn AI pilots into write-offs.
- **If you're a CTO or technical executive:** the architecture stakes, the security and compliance postures, and the signals that separate teams that ship from teams that demo.

## The rules we hold ourselves to

1. **Assumptions are declared, never smuggled.** Every post opens with its scope: what you need to know, what environment we assume, what we deliberately leave out.
2. **Math is derived, not decorated.** Where a formula exists, you get the intuition, the formal statement, the derivation, a worked example with real numbers, and — crucially — where it breaks.
3. **No invented statistics.** Numbers are either computed in front of you or cited to a public source. Examples from "the field" are labeled as the illustrative scenarios they are.
4. **Sources are linked.** Every post ends with curated public resources — documentation, papers, books, videos — so this series can be your single endpoint, not another tab in your pile.
5. **Practice beats prose.** Every post leaves you one concrete thing to run or do.

## The map, briefly

We publish in order, one topic at a time. First, the role itself: what an FDE is, where they come from, what they own, how seniority works, the vocabulary of delivery — delta, UAT, last-mile integration, Day-2 operations. Then the engineering spine: languages, data structures, Git, Linux, the full stack, databases, pipelines. Then the AI core: LLMs, prompting, context engineering, RAG, vector search, agents, MCP. Then production: legacy integration, cloud, containers, security, observability, evaluation, MLOps. And finally the craft that makes it all valuable: discovery, scoping, communication, ROI, and a capstone that ties the system together end to end. The foundations deep-dives follow as a companion track — the mathematical machinery, published in the same discipline.

## A note on tone

This series grew out of an internal project: we write it as a voyage — a crew, a ship, islands of knowledge, a running build that grows from a tiny script into a production system across the posts. You'll catch the flavor in passing. The engineering stays dead serious. If a joke ever carries technical content, we've failed and you should tell us.
